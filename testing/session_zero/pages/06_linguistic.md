### The Serpent's Cadence

**Phonetic Patterns:**
*   **Consonant Tone:** Sibilant and liquid, often sounding like a soft hiss or the rustle of dry leaves. Consonants frequently blend, creating a smooth, almost slithering flow, yet each sound carries a subtle, sharp edge.
*   **Vowel Dominance:** Vowels are clear, crisp, and briefly articulated. They punctuate the dominant sibilant and liquid consonants, providing definition and structure to words without ever being drawn out or sung.
*   **Complexity:** Overwhelmingly monosyllabic, built from blunt, essential roots. Words are short, direct, and efficient, rarely extending beyond a single syllable, even when conveying complex ideas.

**Naming Conventions:**
*   **NPC Names:** Names are typically short, often ending in sibilant or liquid sounds. They are concise, almost like a breath.
    *   **Examples:** Lys, Thys, Kael, Riss, Shol
    *   **Rules:** Names are short, often one syllable, and frequently incorporate 's', 'th', 'l', or 'r' sounds. They feel clipped and direct.
*   **Location Names:** Locations are named with stark, descriptive monosyllables or very short compounds, reflecting their function or a prominent feature. They evoke a sense of the land's harshness or its hidden nature.
    *   **Examples:** Cold-Vein, Slith-Gate, Grim-Lode, Rust-Spire, Whisper-Shaft
    *   **Logic:** Often a single descriptor or two juxtaposed monosyllabic words. The names are utilitarian and hint at underlying unease or ancient presence.
*   **Faction Names:** Faction names are direct and often carry an undertone of subtle authority or a veiled purpose. They are short, impactful, and may allude to the deeper, unsettling elements of the world.
    *   **Examples:** The Cold Hand, The Deep Coil, The Veiled Word, The Grit Cult, The Slithers
    *   **Logic:** Typically two short words, or a single powerful noun, often hinting at their core pursuit or their method.

**Common Idioms & Taboos:**
*   **Sayings:**
    *   "The deep takes what it wills." (A fatalistic acknowledgement of the dangers within the mine.)
    *   "Whispers are sharper than steel." (A warning that subtle information or quiet threats are more potent than overt force.)
    *   "A stone has no tongue, but it sees." (An idiom implying that the ancient, unmoving elements of the world hold secrets or are ever-present observers.)
*   **Linguistic Taboos:**
    *   **The Loud Word:** Speaking in a loud, boisterous, or uncontrolled manner is considered deeply uncouth, almost sacrilegious. True authority is conveyed through precise, quiet command.
    *   **The Old Coil:** Certain ancient, complex words or phrases, believed to belong to a lost empire or the deepest parts of the mine, are strictly forbidden in common speech. To utter them is to invite unknown, unsettling attention.

**Influence on Other DNA:**
This language, with its sibilant commands and clipped words, instills a pervasive sense of unsettling precision. Military orders are delivered as sharp, terse hisses, leaving no room for misinterpretation but carrying a chilling efficiency. Religious texts, rather than being flowery, are stark and rhythmic pronouncements, chanted in a low, sibilant tone that makes liturgy sound like a precise, ancient instruction or a solemn warning. Even everyday conversation is economical and direct, yet often carries a subtle current of veiled meaning or unspoken dread, where a quiet word can hold more weight than a shouted threat. The language itself feels like a constant, low-level whisper of something ancient and powerful just beneath the surface.
