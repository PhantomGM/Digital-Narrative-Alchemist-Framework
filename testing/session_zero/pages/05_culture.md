### **The Shifting Communes**

**A people of:** The wandering reaches of the Saltspire Marches — **herder, commune-bound**

| **Essence**                                       | **Archetype**                     |
| :------------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------- |
| "A people who find divinity in the cast-off and meaning in shared wandering, clinging to old knowledge as they drift." | Nomadic Salvagers of Spirit |

---

### **Overview**
The Shifting Communes are less a single people than a loose constellation of related nomadic groups, each a distinct family of "hulks" – massive, repurposed vehicles or salvaged structures – that traverse the Saltspire Marches. They are easily recognized by the elaborate, often patchwork veils that obscure their faces and bodies, a practical defense against dust and sun, and a symbolic barrier between the individual and the collective. Outsiders often mistake their stoic demeanor for coldness, or their irreverent humor for disrespect, failing to grasp the deep, communal devotion that underpins their way of life. They are a scattered remnant, few in number, yet remarkably open to integrating new members, provided they embrace the communal way.

### **Values & Taboos**
The Communes walk a path woven from apparent contradictions. They are deeply **Stoic**, enduring hardship with a quiet resilience, yet can be surprisingly **Lavish** in their communal celebrations, their storytelling, and the intricate, often salvaged ornamentation woven into their veils and hulks. They are profoundly **Devout**, believing magic to be a divine gift and the world’s cast-off a sacred resource, yet exhibit a blunt **Irreverence** towards grand histories, external authorities, and anything they deem wasteful or prideful.

Their central creed, repeated in countless variations, is: "Waste is the true sin." To squander a resource, to let knowledge fade, or to abandon what might yet serve, is to offend the very fabric of existence.

The most profound **taboo** among the Shifting Communes is **iron**. To wield or craft with iron is to invite ill fortune, for they believe it draws unwanted attention and corrupts the spiritual flow of the land. A transgressor who knowingly works with iron is ritually cleansed, their veil stripped and burned, and then cast out from the commune, left to wander the Marches alone until they can prove their repentance, often by returning with a valuable piece of salvage found without the use of iron tools.

### **Daily Life & Subsistence**
Life in the Shifting Communes revolves around their herds of hardy, long-legged beasts and the perpetual hunt for useful salvage. Their massive "hulks" are often ancient, slow-moving vehicles or converted mining structures, now bristling with salvaged tech that keeps them moving, filters water, or provides light. They are expert **herders**, guiding their flocks across the Marches to seasonal pastures, and master **salvage-tech crafters**, able to coax new life from ancient mechanisms and weave disparate parts into functional wonders. Their most precious resource, however, is **knowledge** – the lore of the old world, the secrets of forgotten machines, the migration patterns of their herds, and the intricate wisdom of survival in a harsh land. This knowledge is carefully guarded, shared, and traded. Their veils are not merely clothing but mobile histories, often embroidered with symbols denoting their commune, their lineage of knowledge, and their personal journeys.

### **Kinship & Society**
Loyalty in the Shifting Communes flows not through bloodlines, but through the **commune** itself – the collective body that shares the hulks, the herds, and the burdens. Traditional family units are present, but individual identity is secondary to the group. Authority is held by the **wise ones** – elders who have accumulated significant knowledge, proven their ability to discern patterns, and demonstrated sound judgment in guiding the commune. These are not leaders in a hierarchical sense, but respected voices whose counsel carries the most weight in discussions. However, with their low cohesion, different communes often disagree on who is truly wise, or how that wisdom should be applied. Disputes are typically settled through long, deliberative discussions among the wise ones, often involving the telling of parables or the citing of past precedents, with the goal of reaching a consensus that serves the commune's survival. Those who cannot agree may simply drift away, forming new splinter communes.

### **Rites & Lifecycle**
For a young member of a Shifting Commune, the **apprenticeship** marks the transition to adulthood. Around the age of ten or twelve, a child is assigned to a wise one or a master crafter, learning a vital skill – herding, navigation, salvage-tech, or lore-keeping. The apprenticeship culminates not in a single ceremony, but in the successful completion of a significant communal task, such as repairing a critical system on a hulk, leading a herd through a difficult season, or discovering a new, valuable piece of knowledge or salvage. Only then are they considered a full, contributing member of the commune.

**Union** is a temporary arrangement, a practical pairing of individuals for mutual support, companionship, and the raising of children within the communal structure. There is no formal ceremony; a pair simply begins to share a space within the hulk, and the commune acknowledges their arrangement. These pairings can dissolve as easily as they form, without shame or stigma, as the primary bond remains with the commune itself.

Death among the Shifting Communes is met with stark **abandonment**. The deceased is removed from the hulk and left where they fall, their body returning to the land as another form of resource. Before this, their veil is carefully removed, cleaned, and offered to the next generation, their personal markings either re-embroidered or covered by new ones, ensuring that nothing is wasted, and the living are not burdened by the dead.

### **Beliefs**
The Communes actively **deny** the notion of a grand "Golden Age" in the past, viewing it as a dangerous distraction or a romanticized lie that leads to waste and regret. They live amidst the ruins of such an age, but see them only as sources of salvage, not as monuments to be revered. They believe **magic is divine**, a manifestation of the world's inherent spirit, a sacred energy that can be channeled and understood through careful observation and the right application of knowledge. Their creed, "Waste is the true sin," extends to the spiritual realm: to waste one's potential, to ignore the divine whispers of magic, or to use it frivolously, is a grave error. They believe true wisdom lies in understanding the interconnectedness of all things and making the most of what is given.

### **Relations with Outsiders**
The Shifting Communes are non-territorial, their hulks and herds drifting across the Marches. They are remarkably open to **adopting** outsiders into their fold, particularly those who demonstrate a willingness to contribute, learn, and abide by the communal creed. They treat strangers with a wary yet pragmatic curiosity, assessing their usefulness and potential for integration. They have no traditional enemies, their scattered nature and focus on survival meaning they rarely engage in sustained conflict. Their **dying language**, a complex, melodic tongue known fully only by the oldest wise ones, is a point of quiet pride and an occasional barrier to communication, though most can speak the common tongue of the Marches.

### **Naming Conventions**
Members of the Shifting Communes are named for a significant **deed, insight, or discovery** they have made, or a notable event in their life. These names are fluid and can change as a person's life progresses or their achievements accumulate.

*   **Marches-Walker:** Someone known for their skill in navigating the Saltspire Marches.
*   **Rust-Whisper:** A skilled salvage-tech crafter who can make dead machines speak.
*   **Veil-Weaver:** One renowned for their intricate veil-making or storytelling through embroidery.
*   **Star-Watcher:** An elder wise one who guides the commune by the celestial patterns.

### **Internal Tensions**
The most potent fault line within the Shifting Communes is the **resource-split**. This tension manifests as a constant debate over how best to utilize their salvaged knowledge and technology. Some wise ones advocate for greater integration with outside settlements, trading their unique salvage-tech for resources they cannot easily acquire, even if it means exposing their dying language or their unique practices. Others argue for a more insular approach, believing that contact with outsiders leads to the dilution of their creed and a greater risk of "waste" – particularly the waste of their unique cultural identity. This disagreement often leads to communes splitting, with one group following the path of cautious engagement and another retreating further into the wilderness, each believing they are upholding the true spirit of "waste is sin."
