### **The Salt-Vent Galley**

**Station mess hall and trade nook** in **Drift-Lock Four** — kept by **Brak-Vat**

### **Atmosphere**
The heavy brass hatch swings open with a soft sigh, releasing a wave of dry heat, roasted grain aromas, and the soothing, steady murmur of quiet chatter. Though crowded shoulder-to-shoulder during major shift-changes, the room maintains a hushed, respectful air. Spacers and rig-welders sit close along bolted zinc tables, leaning together over dented steel mugs. The floorplates hum with a low, comfortable vibration from the reclaimed heat-exchangers humming smoothly underneath the deck.

### **Goods & Services**
Brak-Vat serves piping-hot bowls of thick lentil-and-yeast stew seasoned with precious dried herbs, paired with dense ship-biscuits baked fresh every watch. Off the official inventory log, Brak offers custom-packed salvage rations tailored to individual tastes—dense, nutrient-rich blocks wrapped in grease-paper that keep for weeks in deep cold. A full meal with a steaming cup of scorched-chicory tea costs four scrap-tokens or one undamaged unit of clean rubber gasket sealant.

### **Personnel & Influence**
Brak-Vat works the counter alone, moving between boiling cauldrons and the wash-basin with steady, practiced ease. The crowd includes everyone from grease-stained cutters to senior representatives of the Rig-Haul Guild. Because senior crew members and station decision-makers regularly pull up stools here for breakfast, more sensible station business gets sorted over Brak's soup than in any formal council room.

### **Secrets & Threats**
Behind the flour sacks in the dry pantry lies a warm, concealed crawlway hatch where Brak nurtures a thriving, off-manifest culture of sweet basil and real sourdough yeast. The room has no heavy armed security; instead, off-duty members of the Vent-Keepers lounge near the door, keeping a watchful eye out to ensure no one ruins the only peaceful place on the deck. The only hazard in the house is a temperamental steam manifold above the main cauldron, which spits a sudden puff of hot vapor unless you tap the iron casing twice before opening the valve.

### **Interdependencies**
The galley relies on long-haul traders moving through Grave-Span Nine to trade off-world spice packets for hot meals. Brak also keeps an informal arrangement with the station quartermaster, exchanging fresh sourdough bread for priority access to clean water filters. If incoming trade routes freeze or station water allocations are cut, the kettle goes cold, stripping Drift-Lock Four of its central community post.

### **Evolution Over Time**
Originally an automated emergency-ration vault, the space was lovingly converted by Brak-Vat over eight years of relentless scavenging and weld-work. It has quietly evolved from a basic food stop into the station’s vital civic hearth, where old feuds are talked out over warm bread before they ever reach a cutter's deck.

### **Adventure Hooks**
* **The Kettle's Valve:** Brak-Vat needs a specific heat-resistant brass fitting to fix the sputtering kettle manifold—a part only found intact aboard the derelict freight-hulk *Iron-Vent Eight*. He offers three weeks of custom long-haul rations for anyone who can bring one back clean.
* **Quiet Mediation:** Two rival cutter crews are on the verge of a territorial dispute over a salvage run. Brak asks the party to sit with them at the corner table, buy a round of tea, and help them talk through an even split before tempers flare outside the airlock.
