### **Overview**
The Spire-Tenders are the long-haul scavengers and deck-dwellers of the dead corridors. They do not regard a vessel as a vehicle to get from one station to another; to a Tender, a ship or a derelict frame *is* home. An outsider's first impression is often one of cold, mechanical distance — masked figures in patched canvas suits moving through ruined hulls with silent, practiced choreography. Strangers frequently mistake them for grim, unfeeling scrap-monsters who pick over graves. In truth, they are a warm, rhythm-obsessed people who fill their echo-prone habitats with whistle-songs, valve-knocks, and fiercely defended communal tea-kells.

### **Values & Taboos**
They honour physical endurance, deck hierarchy, and absolute fidelity to a sworn berth-contract. To a Tender, metal fatigue is a spiritual truth: everything gives way under pressure eventually, so you respect the load-bearers while they stand. 

Their central taboo is **The Bare Face**. To expose an unfiltered, unmasked face in shared deck-space or before strangers is considered a shocking breach of decency and safety — akin to stepping into a public corridor bleeding freely from an unstitched seam. Those who drop their mask in public without emergency cause are isolated in dry-dock scrubbers until the guild elders are satisfied they respect the shared air.

Their common caution runs: *"Keep your seal tight and your cut clean; the hull remembers who left the scorch."*

### **Daily Life & Subsistence**
Tenders live in "spires" — towering, vertical habitations constructed by docking half a dozen modular salvage tenders around a central lattice column or dead structural spar. They dress in thick, plain-woven canvas over-suits, grease-dyed aprons, and soft-soled boots that grip cold deck-plates without ringing out. 

Their primary craft is acoustic pipe-music and hull-tuning. Using brass whistles, tuned copper rods, and rhythmic tap-hammers, they diagnose structural stress in derelicts by sound alone, turning maintenance into elaborate percussion performances. Their entire economy turns on **scrubbed water**. Ice-harvesting and grey-water reclamation are wealth; a debt is measured, lent, and repaid in liters of purified tank-water.

### **Kinship & Society**
Traditional bloodlines carry little weight. The fundamental social unit is the **berth-pact** — a guild bond forged between crewmates who share a work-deck, a water-ration, and a salvage vessel. Children born on the tenders are raised collectively by the shift-pact as apprentices, taking on the specialty of whichever master cutter or valve-smith takes them under wing.

Authority is held not by captains or lords, but by **Deck-Elders** — old hands who have survived decades of hull-breaches and reactor drops, whose wisdom is measured in the scar-lines on their suit-gaskets. They are far from unified; independent spire-clans and long-haul fleets argue endlessly over scrap rights, valve-maintenance duties, and water-ice routes. Disputes are settled through formal acoustic arbitrations or pipe-duels, where guild representatives demonstrate their knowledge of hull mechanics before neutral elders.

### **Rites & Lifecycle**
* **Coming-of-Age:** The **First-Salvage**. A youth becomes a recognized deck-hand when they board an unscrubbed derelict alone, successfully locate and detach a usable mechanical component — a water valve, an intact loom, a copper bus-bar — and return to the tender without damaging their suit or panicking in the dark.
* **Union:** **Berth-Contracts**. Pairings are temporary cohabitation agreements signed for the duration of a specific salvage run, haul season, or five-year maintenance cycle. When the contract expires, the couple may renew it, adjust their water shares, or part ways with a shared brew and no hard feelings.
* **Death Rite:** **The Abandonment**. When a Tender dies, their body is dressed in their patched work-suit, stamped with a copper name-plate, and left inside the heart of a cleared salvage hull or released toward the rim. They are left to rest with the steel that fed them.

### **Beliefs**
They scoff at the notion of a past "Golden Age." To the Spire-Tenders, the ancients were arrogant fools who built magnificent, fragile monsters and lacked the good sense to recycle their grey-water. 

They view the strange arcana and anomalous pressures of the deep reach as raw physical forces — high-voltage lines or pressurized lines that simply require the right bypass valve and proper grounding. Their core creed is that **names hold power**. Every ship frame, valve, cutting torch, and person has an earned name stamped into registry. Calling a machine or a crewmate by a false name invites misfires and hull-cackle.

### **Relations with Outsiders**
Outsiders are met with quiet caution. Tenders trade selectively at station hubs, swapping salvaged alloy, copper bus-bars, and scrubbed water for grain and filter-resins, but they rarely invite strangers past the airlocks of their spires. They speak **Tap-Cant** (or Hull-Cant), a dialect born from suit-knocks, valve-clicks, and rhythmic pipe-taps that can pass through solid bulkheads when radio comms fail. 

They hold a deep, burning grudge against several station syndicates following an incident where a station customs authority forcibly unmasked a detained salvage crew during a tax inspection — an outrage that resulted in three guilds severing trade with the sector for a decade.

### **Naming Conventions**
Tenders earn their names through deck roles, haul history, or memorable repair jobs, appended to a short birth-call.

* **Sola Pipe-Tuned** (a quiet environmental mechanic who likes brewing mint tea)
* **Kael Three-Pump** (a veteran salvage diver known for fixing clogged bilge-lines)
* **Vane Iron-Scribe** (a manifest clerk who keeps trade accounts stamped on scrap-plates)
* **Miren Cold-Berth** (a long-haul pilot who keeps a collection of dried flowers in her cockpit)

### **Internal Tensions**
A deep rift is growing between the **Station-Anchors** — who want to lock their habit-spires to fixed trading outposts and trade water freely with outsiders — and the **Deep-Haulers**, who insist that staying anchored breeds soft seals, rots the water tanks, and leaves the guilds reliant on people who don't know how to handle a wrench.
