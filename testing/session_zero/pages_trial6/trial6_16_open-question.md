### **the-testament**

**Kind:** legend — **kept by elders of the line, carried as mural-work**
**Held:** common across specific mid-tier salvage berths, defended with quiet, stubborn pride rather than violent zeal.

| **Essence**                        | **Archetype**              |
| :--------------------------------- | :------------------------- |
| "The ledger of the first weld remains unbroken, and the third line shall return to claim the tonnage." | The foundational charter of legitimate succession. |

---

### **What Is Claimed**
The lineage of the early freight syndicates did not scatter into the void when the central registries failed during the early expansion drifts; rather, it was gathered and held in trust. The elders teach that the original chartering families of the Vesper Reach left a direct line of inheritance carved into the bulkheads of the oldest drydocks — a bloodline promise that guarantees whoever holds the verified name and the matching seal holds absolute priority over unclaimed hulks in Grave-Span Nine. 

Believers recite the foundational verse at every shift change or when sealing a new salvage claim:
> *"The iron bears the signature, the seal is struck in deep-drawn lead, and when the third generation turns the valve, the vault-strip falls to rightful hand."*

### **Who Keeps It**
The keepers are the senior deck-elders and lineage-reciters stationed at Drift-Lock Four and various quieter berths along the Corridor. They transmit the legend through large, layered wall-murals painted in faded zinc and engine-black across communal bulkheads. The observance requires a daily recitation of the names before any cutting torch is lit on a new run. This tradition authorizes the established lineage families to demand priority docking rights, first-pass salvage shares, and a minor registration fee from independent crews working the deeper sectors.

### **Who Disputes It**
Independent crews, free-haulers, and members of competing syndicates like the Choke-Weld Line view the legend as a self-serving fabrication designed to protect old monopolies. They argue that the original charters were nullified when the bulkheads were abandoned decades ago, and that salvage belongs to whoever burns through the hatch first. Saying so aloud in certain station galleys will cost a crew a friendly cup of tea or a helpful tip on a fresh hulk, but doubt itself carries no formal penalty or shunning.

### **What Is Actually True**
The core record of the original family registries is fundamentally accurate, tracing back to the earliest days of corporate expansion in the Reach. However, whether the current claimants are direct biological or structural descendants of those original signatories is entirely unknowable. The physical proof—an ancient, hardened lead sealing-matrix containing the master stamps—was buried or sealed within the bulkheads of a derelict hulk long before current records began, and no living person knows which drifting mass holds the vault. Because the evidence is permanently sealed away in the deep void, the matter cannot be settled by any ledger review or pressure test.

### **Variants & Drift**
A literalist split has formed among the younger haulers and outer-rim factions, who insist that the testament applies only to physical machinery and automated drydocks rather than human bloodlines. In addition, decades of repainting and corridor repairs have led to a specific corruption in the murals: a prominent founding surname was entirely struck out after a forgotten schism, leaving a blank space where a crucial ancestor's name once anchored the middle of the chant.

### **In Play**
* **A Rival's Proof:** During a standard sweep of Iron-Vent Eight, a crew member uncovers a heavy, cold-stamped lead matrix wedged behind an auxiliary conduit—bearing the exact struck-out name from the murals, suggesting the disowned lineage might actually have a valid claim to the entire sector's tonnage.
* **The Broken Recitation:** An aging keeper aboard Drift-Lock Four forgets a crucial verse of the daily recitation during a critical docking procedure, leading superstitious deckhands to refuse the airlock seal until the sequence is corrected.
