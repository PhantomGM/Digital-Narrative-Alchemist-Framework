### **Description**

**Body & Movement**
A single Grave-Flicker is no larger than a deck-bolt, possessing a segmented, translucent shell that bends light like fractured glass. Floating weightlessly through zero-gravity passages, it uses papery bio-mechanical cilia to catch air drafts or weak magnetic currents from unshielded wiring. Lacking eyes, it navigates entirely by sensing thermal gradients across metal plating and living bodies.
*Sensory Tell:* Before a pack becomes visible, the ambient temperature in a compartment drops rapidly—enough to bead frost across a visor—accompanied by a faint, metallic rattling akin to dry seeds shaking inside a steel pipe.

**Behaviour & Hunting**
Driven by a mechanical instinct to feed on heat, Grave-Flickers show the coordinated, tactical patience of hunting pack animals. They do not strike with fangs or claws; instead, they drift onto warm targets—engine manifolds, exposed power cables, or the heated seams of a pressure suit—and anchor themselves with microscopic chitin hooks. Once attached, they absorb heat directly into their crystalline cores, dropping local hull temperatures below freezing within minutes.
When startled, a pack exhibits a signature evasion reflex: they simultaneously dump their stored thermal energy in a blinding burst of infrared illumination, dazzling optical sensors and thermal visors while they scatter into dark pipe-work.

**Ecology**
Grave-Flickers build communal clusters in deep, unlit conduit junctions where remnant ship power still hums. They multiply by secreting sticky, resinous egg-nodes directly onto thermal exchanger coils. These nodes can lie dormant for decades until a salvage crew's torches or boot-thrusters warm the surrounding bulkhead. Once a widespread pest during the early expansion of Grave-Span Nine, their population has steadily declined as older wrecks go completely cold, leaving isolated clusters trapped in forgotten hulks.

**The Anomaly**
The origin of the species remains an unresolved debate among station mechanics. Internal dissections reveal a pre-collapse nanite lattice, yet their crystalline husks incorporate raw mineral structures typically associated with ambient aetherium deposits. Whether they began as artificial heat-regulation micro-drones that mutated or wild pests that adapted to starship architecture remains unknown.

### **Gamemaster's Toolkit**

**Threat & Tactics**
Grave-Flickers rely on stealth and environment rather than brute force. They anchor near airlocks and primary breaker panels, waiting for a crew to restore power or fire up cutting rigs. Once active, they descend in small waves, prioritizing suit heaters and portable battery banks. A crew caught unawares will notice suit power dropping rapidly before discovering their gear is dusted in frost-covered parasites.

**Weakness**
The delicate nanite matrix inside their cilia reacts violently to basic sodium compounds. Spraying a cluster with standard deck-deicing brine or industrial salt slurry disrupts their thermal draw, causing them to detach, drop from the plating, and shatter against the deck.

**Signs of Its Presence**
* Rings of geometric frost forming around active hatch motors or suit power packs.
* Rapid, unexplained power loss in portable tools left resting against structural iron.
* A soft, rhythmic clicking echoing through cold ventilation ducts.

**Salvage**
Intact husks can be gathered using insulated tongs. When crushed, the resulting crystalline powder—known among riggers as *charged motes*—is purchased by station spark-welders for use as a high-efficiency thermal flux or micro-battery additive.

**System-Agnostic Mechanical Note**
*Thermal Leech:* When a pack attaches to a character or vehicle, it inflicts no physical wounds. Instead, it degrades environmental heat shielding and drains one charge of battery power or life-support reserve every minute until removed or sprayed with brine. Characters whose power is depleted face environmental exposure hazards rather than immediate impact damage.
