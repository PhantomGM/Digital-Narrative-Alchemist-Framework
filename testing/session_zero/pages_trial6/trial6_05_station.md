### **Drift-Lock Four**

### Overview
Drift-Lock Four is a sprawling, multi-ringed orbital station tethered to the iron skeleton of a forgotten pre-collapse dreadnought. Positioned at the mouth of Grave-Span Nine, it serves as the primary staging ground for salvage crews venturing into the dead corridor. Officially classified as a temporary refueling stop by distant transit authorities, the reality is a densely packed, living habitat that has outgrown its structural frame. Its inner corridors are packed tight with press-fitted living quarters and hum with life, while its massive outer drydocks offer top-tier hull repair facilities even as its defensive turrets sit half-rigged with salvaged wiring.

### Physical Description
The station’s core architecture is built from industrial-grade plating and heavy-gauge structural bulkheads designed to endure centuries of micrometeoroid impacts. Walkways are polished smooth by generations of rubber-soled boots, and central plazas feature massive, reinforced observation domes looking out into the void. Despite the polished brass fittings and high-end air scrubber assemblies in the central concourse, the outer hull presents a battered, scorched facade. Docking gantries extend like rusted fingers, patched with mismatched plating from a dozen different ship classes. Steam vents hiss continuously along the maintenance spines, and lighting flickers in predictable patterns as power cycles between the heavy forge bays and the residential rings.

### Population
Life on Drift-Lock Four is crowded and loud. Air tastes faintly of recycled ozone and toasted grain, a testament to the heavy scrubbers and ubiquitous hydroponic bakeries. Because space is at a premium, residents share tight living quarters and tight shifts, giving rise to an intense, defensive local pride. The populace maintains a sharp, no-nonsense exterior toward outsiders, yet holds a deep commitment to internal mutual aid—no one goes without a hot meal or a sealed suit when a bulkhead leaks. Public health is solid thanks to clean water recycling, though minor bumps and bruises from shipboard labor are universal. Street crime in the formal sense is rare; disputes are settled directly in dockside rings or over steaming mugs in the mess halls.

### Economy
The lifeblood of Drift-Lock Four is the salvage trade. Heavy industrial tenders, plasma welding bays, and hull refitting rigs operate around the clock, converting raw scrap dragged out of the corridor into structural beams, replacement airlocks, and drive couplings. Formal financial institutions do not exist here; instead, commerce runs on credit ledgers backed by fuel mass, spare parts, and atmospheric scrub cartridges. While formal merchant guilds have failed to take root, independent mechanics and master fitters command huge respect. Premium repair services and custom engineering are easy to find, provided a crew brings usable metal or rare tech to the forge master.

### Politics & Law
Governance on the station is minimal and largely informal. A appointed harbor administrator manages dock assignments and emergency airlock protocols, but real authority rests with informal hall committees formed by senior crew chiefs and station engineers. Station rules are straightforward and practical: don't vent atmosphere, don't mess with the power grid, and keep the docking lanes clear. Corruption manifests not as bribes for malice, but as back-channel favors—a chief engineer might look the other way on an unregistered berth in exchange for a crate of fresh synthetic gaskets. This lack of rigid oversight keeps the station flexible and welcoming to independent salvage tenders, even if official records lag months behind reality.

### Notable Locations
*   **The Copper Scupper:** A sprawling, split-level tavern built inside an inverted cargo hold. Filtered tea, hot broth, and fermented mash are served in heavy ceramic mugs. A massive viewport looks directly into the primary salvage bay, making it the premier spot for off-duty crews to critique incoming hauls.
*   **Bay Seven Refitting Dock:** A massive, climate-controlled workspace run by veteran fitters. Capable of housing medium tenders, it features heavy overhead gantry cranes, precision plasma cutters, and automated hull-seal injectors.
*   **The Wire-Spire Market:** A narrow vertical shaft lined with metal stalls where tech-brokers and scrap-merchants trade salvaged circuit boards, sensor arrays, and restored nav-minds.

### Surroundings
Drift-Lock Four floats at the stable Lagrange point of a barren dust moon, flanked by field after field of dormant debris fields and ancient navigation buoys. Nearby lie smaller, derelict hulks used for extra storage or targeted target practice, while the mouth of Grave-Span Nine yawns just beyond the local sensor range. Farther out along the corridor sit abandoned waypoints and cold mining outposts, making Drift-Lock Four the last guaranteed warm hearth before entering the deep dark.

### Trajectory
While the station’s footprint remains anchored to its original dreadnought core, its strategic importance in the region is climbing rapidly. As salvage operations deeper in Grave-Span Nine yield valuable components, more independent crews are adopting Drift-Lock Four as their permanent home base. The physical space is reaching its absolute limit, leading engineering crews to plan new hull extensions using salvaged plates from incoming wrecks.

### Hooks & Opportunities
*   A newly arrived salvage haul in Bay Seven contains a sealed, unmapped navigational vault from an old freighter, but the crew who brought it in lacks the specialized tools to open it without melting the drive core.
*   The station’s primary water scrubber is running hot due to a missing manifold seal that can only be matched by parts from a specific class of derelict vessel last seen drifting near Cold-Spur Bank.
*   Two rival salvage tenders are disputing ownership of a floating fuel barge anchored just outside the station's perimeter, and the hall committee needs an impartial crew to inspect the barge’s log-seal.
