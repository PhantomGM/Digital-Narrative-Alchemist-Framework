## **The Cold-Drift Bypass**

## Travel Overview
Threaded through the shadow-cast wake of Grave-Span Nine, this wide corridor skirts the main traffic lanes between Drift-Lock Four and the outer hulk fields. It is a quiet expanse of space where major commerce rarely passes, but the debris density is remarkably high. Navigators along this run spend little time watching for pirate thermal blooms, but their eyes are glued to the broad-spectrum scanners—the vacuum here is littered with historical wrecks, adrift cargo pods, and forgotten sensor buoys, inviting frequent detours for a crew willing to burn a little extra fuel to poke at the past.

## Route Options

### The Outer Shallows
- **Time**: 5 Days
- **Navigation DC**: 12 (Wisdom / Systems)
- **Resources DC**: 14 (Intelligence / Engineering)
- **Terrain Features**: Low-density micro-meteoroid belts, wide visual clearance, predictable thermal currents.
- **Pros & Cons**: Safe and steady with minimal hull stress, but consumes two extra days of life support and thruster propellant.

### The Flare-Gut Cut
- **Time**: 3 Days
- **Navigation DC**: 17 (Wisdom / Systems)
- **Resources DC**: 18 (Intelligence / Engineering)
- **Terrain Features**: Ionized dust ribbons, heavy solar particle streams, electromagnetic blind spots.
- **Pros & Cons**: Cuts travel time significantly, but risks frying sensor arrays and draining radiation shielding reserves.

## Encounters

### The Skiff-Rider’s Signal
A low-frequency distress ping leads to a lone, rusted salvage skiff drifting on auxiliary battery power. Its pilot, a freelance prospector named Vex-Thorne, is uninjured but out of reaction mass after over-throttling away from a shifting debris cluster. He offers the coordinates of an unlooted cargo transport's bridge deck in exchange for three units of RCS propellant and a hot meal in your galley.

### Ionized Dust Ribbon
A shimmering cloud of charged metallic silt drifts directly across the flight path. Entering without grounding the outer hull will scorch radiator vanes and disrupt main sensor calibration, forcing the crew to choose between a costly four-hour detour or riding out the static storm while taking minor hull pitting.

### The Tug-Weight Dilemma
*Journey-Changer*  
The crew detects a fully intact, automated heavy haul-rig drifting aimlessly with its engine locks active. It holds eight industrial-grade seal-gaskets and a crate of unrefined copper wire—a windfall for station repairs. However, its steering thrusters are dead. Towing the heavy rig back to Drift-Lock Four will double your fuel consumption for the remainder of the trip and increase transit time by one full day, forcing a hard choice about your remaining supplies.

## Discoveries

### The Glass-Eye Relay
A dead long-range communications spire from an early survey expedition floats silently in the dark. A quick EVA scan reveals its local data core is still powered by a trickling isotope generator. Downloading the logs reveals an old star-chart fragment pointing toward a buried hull section deep in the span, alongside thirty years of automated solar weather records.

### The Scrap-Cluster Arc
A strange, bloom-like formation of four atmospheric shuttle hulls, fused together at the sterns by an ancient welding mishap. Tethering up and boarding the central airlock reveals a functional, industrial-scale water-purification filter cartridge—a rare find that can significantly reduce life-support recycling costs back at the station.

## Journey Narrative Integration (Getting There is Half the Fun)

### Dramatic Journey Moment
On the third day, a sudden thermal solar flare forces the ship to power down non-essential systems and drift silent and dark inside the shadow of a hollowed-out freighter hull for six hours. Inside the dimmed flight deck, the silence is total save for the rhythmic creak of cooling hull plates, forcing the crew to sit together in the amber glow of battery-powered emergency lanterns, sharing a single pot of rehydrated tea while watching the solar curtain dance across the viewscreen.

### Memorable Scene
Passing through a field of ruptured coolant tanks where tiny frozen ammonia crystals float like a million silver needles against the blackness. As the ship’s maneuvering thrusters hit the field, the crystals flash white in the exhaust light, creating the illusion of flying through a blizzard in deep space.

## Travel Moments: Camp, Night, Lost

### Setting Camp: Power-Down Drift
When locking down for a eight-hour rest shift, the crew must choose between maintaining full climate control (spending extra fuel cell charges) or dropping heat to baseline levels and sleeping in thermal suits. Choosing the colder rest leaves everyone shivering over breakfast, but saves critical energy for the return leg.

### The Night: Watch Shifts and Hull-Pings
During the third watch, the light, irregular *tink-tink-tink* of microscopic grit hitting the forward viewport acts as a rhythm for quiet conversation. It is a moment for two crewmates to discuss a long-deferred repair project, trade home-world stories, or quietly check the seals on their EVA suits without the pressure of active salvage operations.

### Getting Lost: Sensor-Wash Out
An unexpected surge of magnetosphere static from a nearby gas giant blinds the primary astrogation suite. The ship strays twenty degrees off course before the error is caught. To regain alignment without burning extra fuel, the navigator must climb into the manual cupola and take optical sightings on fixed radio-spires using a grease pencil and a hand-held sight grid, while the pilot gently nudges the RCS thrusters.

## Special Conditions
A persistent solar particle stream sweeps through this sector every twenty-four hours. Each day at cycle-turn, the ship must make a routine environmental check (Wisdom or Intelligence DC 13). Success means shielding holds and thermal systems compensate smoothly; failure results in temporary sensor interference (disadvantage on navigation checks for the next 8 hours) or a minor drain on primary battery banks.

## Mechanical Guidelines

- **Pace Options**:
  - *Slow*: 2/3 speed, +4 to Navigation checks, reduced fuel consumption (saves 1 unit of fuel per 2 days).
  - *Medium*: Standard travel speed and resource usage.
  - *Fast*: 4/3 speed, -4 to Navigation checks, no resource scanning/foraging permitted, double thruster fuel burn.
- **Encounter Check**: Roll 6d6 daily; any result of 1 triggers an encounter.
- **Discovery Check**: Roll 1d6 daily; any result of 6 or lower triggers a discovery point.
- **Navigation**: Wisdom (Systems/Survival) against route Navigation DC (rolled secretly by GM).
- **Resource Recovery**: Intelligence (Engineering) or Wisdom (Survival) against route Resource DC to scavenge fuel, parts, or water from debris fields.
- **Lost Progress**: On a failed Navigation check, no forward distance is made for 12 hours while the ship recalibrates its location.

## Story Hooks

- **The Rogue Beacon**: A forgotten distress frequency activates on a military channel last used twenty years ago during the corridor blockade.
- **Toll-Collector's Marker**: A drifting marker buoy broadcasts a automated message demanding a fuel tax on behalf of a long-defunct salvage union.
- **The Floating Ledger**: The frozen corpse of a crew master drifting in an undamaged suit, holding a sealed data-slate marked with the registry of a lost payroll vessel.
