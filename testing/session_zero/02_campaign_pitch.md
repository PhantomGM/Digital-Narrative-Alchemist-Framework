# THE HOLLOW ASSAY — campaign pitch

*Assembled from the 15 generated entities. Every name, faction and NPC below came
out of the DNA pipeline under the Session 0 contract; nothing here was written by
hand except the ordering.*

---

## The place

**Deepstone Bastion** is an ancient imperial fortress that lost its empire. Its
walls were built to garrison an army; they now shelter a frontier mining town
that sprawled up inside them. Vast echoing chambers stand empty behind streets
that are loud and crowded. It is a grand structure slowly succumbing to decay and
brimming with new life at the same time, held together by a web of corruption
that — against all odds — keeps it stable.

It clings to the slopes of the **Saltspire Marches**, dust country, crossed by the
**Shifting Communes**: veiled nomadic salvagers who live in convoys of repurposed
hulks and find divinity in cast-off things.

Beneath the town square, its entrance gaping in the middle of everything, is
**The Maw** — the Deepstone Bastion Mine. Still working. Still profitable. Its
shafts go further down than any surviving survey admits.

## The trouble

Three powers hold incompatible claims on it, and they do not even want the same
kind of thing:

- **The Whispering Descent** — eldritch scholars who live in the uncharted deep
  and are indifferent to the surface except where it interferes. They want what is
  down there and will pay any price to reach it.
- **The Obsidian Vigil** — a clandestine order that wants the shaft sealed. They
  are blamed for accidents and closures, and they deserve some of the blame. They
  believe they are the only thing standing between the town and something creeping.
- **The Loom Syndicate** — civic architects who are frankly uninterested in the
  mine. They want *Deepstone Bastion*: its order, its economy, its people running
  smoothly. The mine matters to them only as an input.

Backing any one of them closes doors with the other two. There is no neutral seat.

## What is wrong

People who go too deep come back subtly changed. The cause is small: the
**Aether-Motes**, no larger than a thumbnail, whose ecological impact is out of all
proportion to their size. They drain vitality. They spread through the tunnels in
crystalline clutches. Nobody in town agrees on what they are.

Underneath all of it sits **The Question** — a judgment the Obsidian Vigil renders
on claims of lineage and inheritance, decoded from an ancient coded text by a
humming machine only their Echo-Speaker may tend. To be judged falsely is to lose
your name. What almost nobody knows is that the text is genuine and the truth it
identifies is a deliberate ancient forgery. Who forged it, and why, is not settled
and may never be.

## Who you will keep meeting

- **Kaelen, Archivist of Echoes** — town historian. Absolutely honest, absolutely
  responsible, and entirely without warmth. Will sacrifice one person for ten and
  sleep fine.
- **Morwen of the Sunken Paths** — protects a hidden community of outcasts down in
  the deep. Unyielding benevolence toward her own people, and a bloody toll for
  anyone wanting in.
- **Seraphina Volkov** — Chief Magistrate. An instrument of order, haunted by the
  arithmetic her office requires of her.

## Where it starts

Kaelen asks you to go down. A cartographer named Master Elara — who reads the
mine's energies rather than seeing it — went after a strong signature in the lower
veins. A series of recent rockfalls shifted the workings and she has not come back
out. The Loom Syndicate wants her maps. The Obsidian Vigil has expressed
"interest." Kaelen wants her alive.

You are going into The Maw in the first hour of the first session, and everyone
who is asking you to do it wants something different from what you find.
