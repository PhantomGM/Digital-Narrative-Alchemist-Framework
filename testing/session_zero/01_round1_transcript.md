# Session 0 — Round 1 (private, independent)

Four player-agents answered a seven-question Session 0 questionnaire without
seeing each other's answers. Private submission is deliberate: it mirrors real
safety-tool practice and it is the condition under which the private-veil problem
can surface at all.

---

## Elias — Actor / Storyteller, 4 years

1. High fantasy with real gothic edges — old bloodlines, crumbling houses, a world with history pressing down on it. Warm and funny one scene, genuinely unsettling the next. Cozy mystery energy for the in-between: a town, recurring faces, secrets that unspool slowly.
2. Talking. A long wagon ride where the party gets into what they believe; talking an NPC down instead of drawing steel. Relationships that develop, not just quest-givers. "I'm not counting initiative rounds as the fun part."
3. Death on the table — "if it can't happen, nothing feels like it costs anything." Fair = foreshadowing and agency. "A death that caps an arc is a gift. A death from a trap nobody could've seen coming is just annoying."
4. Nothing to add. **"Don't make me ask for a fade-to-black as a special favour in the moment — if we're heading toward a Veil, just take the transition as a given and keep moving."**
5. Out loud is fine for him. Wants an X-card available **for other people's sake, not his own**.
6. Great = still thinking about what his character would say after the table breaks up. Disengages when it becomes pure tactical combat with roleplay as garnish, or his choices stop mattering to the plot.
7. **Never got to play a full redemption arc for someone the party initially distrusted.**

## Sarah — Tactician / Problem-Solver, 10+ years

1. High fantasy or hard sci-fi, but **dense** — factions with real competing interests, a world that keeps running whether or not we poke it. "Stakes that are structural, not just big monster."
2. Tactical combat and untangling political messes, roughly equal. Fights where the map matters. Scenes reading a room, deciding which faction to burn. Not here for exploration or crafting for its own sake.
3. Death on the table. Not save-or-die. "Fair death is one we saw coming and chose to risk anyway. Cheap death is a bad dice streak on turn one with no choices attached."
4. **Critical clarification: the bigotry Line applies to the SETTING, not only to scenes involving her PC.** "I don't want homophobia, transphobia, or racism built into the world's cultures as background flavour even if no one's PC ever encounters it directly — I'd rather the factions be awful for reasons that aren't real-world bigotry copy-pasted in."
5. Out loud fine; private message to GM if it needs a beat to sort out first.
6. Great = the political and combat threads end up connected. Disengages when combat is a formality with a foregone conclusion, or the plot is on rails.
7. **Never got to run a genuinely three-sided conflict where the party picks a side or brokers, with real mechanical consequences later.**

## Marcus — Explorer / Instigator, 1.5 years

1. "Starving" for cyberpunk or weird west. Neon and rain and corporate conspiracies, **or dusty towns with something wrong under the floorboards.** Dark and tense but with room for banter. Not grimdark 24/7.
2. Exploring, hands down. "A door I haven't opened, a terminal I haven't hacked, a map with a blank spot on it." Will press the button when the party stalls.
3. Death on the table for real, no plot armour. Fair = a choice led there. "If I do something reckless and it kills me, that's actually kind of awesome to me."
4. **Clarifies the animal Line: "it's less 'don't include animals' and more please don't make me watch a pet or companion creature die on screen."** An on-screen-event constraint, not a content-existence one.
5. Quick out-of-character heads up. "I'm pretty comfortable just saying it."
6. Great = a story he wants to retell, and at least one "oh SHIT" reveal that recontextualised things. Disengages if sessions become 90% shopping/logistics/table admin with no forward momentum.
7. **Never got to play slow-burn sci-fi horror / body horror — "something is deeply wrong with this ship/town."**

## Chloe — Audience / Support, 6 months

1. Cozy mystery, or space opera found-family "bumbling through the galaxy together." High fantasy works too. No strong preference. "I just want something warm where the group feels like a team."
2. Talking and exploring. "Combat's fine but I'm not there for the tactics, I'm there to keep everyone alive and cheer when someone does something awesome."
3. Death if it matters. Defers heavily: "I trust the GM's judgment on this way more than my own."
4. Restates her Lines firmly. Romance and gaslighting off-screen — "doesn't need to be cut, just don't need to watch it play out."
5. **"Honestly this is the part I'm least sure about. I don't think I'd feel comfortable just saying it out loud in the moment, especially being newer to the table — I'd probably freeze up."** Wants a private message to the GM, or a silent X-card. "I'd just want it to not be a big dramatic thing."
6. Great = felt part of the team, got to react to the chaos. Disengages if she's filler, **or if every session is heavy intense drama with no room to just enjoy being there.**
7. **Never got to lean into a support character and have it feel impactful — "actually save someone at the right moment."**
