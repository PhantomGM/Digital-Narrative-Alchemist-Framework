### **The Solder-Kin**

**A people of:** The Dead Corridor and the deep hulks — **Salvage-miners, Covenant-bound**

### **Overview**
To the merchants at Tether-End, the Solder-Kin are silent, intimidating shapes clad in scuffed pressure suits, their faces hidden behind gold-mirrored visors. They are often dismissed as mere scavengers—grim, robotic workers who strip the marrow from dead starships and vanish back into the dark. 

But this is a complete misunderstanding. Step past the airlocks of their home-vessels, into the pressurized "hearth-chambers" where the helmets come off, and you will find an intensely expressive, loud, and musical people. They do not view the void as an empty desert, but as a vast cathedral of cold iron, and themselves as its caretakers. They live within the hollowed-out hulls of massive, drifting derelicts, turning dead metal into warm, vibrant homes.

### **Values & Taboos**
The Solder-Kin are bound by a deep, religious reverence for the structure of ships and the integrity of sealed systems. They are fiercely frugal, treating the waste of a single copper washer as a minor sin, yet they are remarkably merciful to those stranded in the deep, always willing to share their air and water.

Their central taboo is **the Bare Face**. To expose one's bare skin or face to an outsider, or to the unshielded void outside of a ship’s consecrated hearth-chamber, is a profound spiritual violation. It is seen as letting the raw, corrupting void—and the spiritual rot of those who live on planets—seep into the soul. A Solder-Kin who willfully bares their face to an outsider is "un-soldered." They are cast out of the covenant, their name is ground off the ship’s hull-plates, and they are forced to live in exile, wearing a featureless, blank mask that can never be removed.

> *"Keep the seal tight, child. A leaky joint lets the rot in, and a bare face lets the soul pour out."*

### **Daily Life & Subsistence**
The life of the Solder-Kin turns entirely on scrap. They mine the structural ribs of dead dreadnoughts, salvage ancient reactors, and filter frozen fuel from drifting tanks. 

They dress in heavy, patchwork environment suits, heavily customized with brass-wire embroidery and copper-plated joints. Underneath their suits, their skin is a dense canvas of dark, intricate tattoos. These markings are not merely decorative; they are technical schematics, lineage charts, and records of every successful salvage run they have survived.

While they make very little that terrestrial societies value, they are masters of a unique form of acoustic craft. They create complex, resonant percussion instruments from scrap metal and ship bulkheads, using them to perform massive, echoing vocal choruses that reverberate through the structural frames of their ships.

### **Kinship & Society**
Traditional blood-families are absent among the Solder-Kin. Instead, they are organized into covenants—sworn pacts of mutual survival tied to a specific salvage-vessel. Children are raised communally by the entire crew, but a person's true loyalty is to the hull they serve.

Authority is held by the **Elders of the Weld**. These are the oldest, most experienced navigators and master-scrappers who have survived decades of radiation and void-work. They do not rule by decree, but by interpreting the structural health of the ship and arbitrating the strict ledger of the salvage-share. 

Though they present a united, intensely uniform front to outsiders, they are not a monolith. The Solder-Kin are divided into rival ship-clans who constantly bicker over salvage rights along the Iron Ribbon. They will race each other to claim a newly discovered hulk, using non-lethal boarding maneuvers, structural jamming, and creative sabotage to secure the prize.

### **Rites & Lifecycle**
* **The Naming-Rite (Coming-of-Age):** A youth is not considered a full member of the covenant until they perform their first "cold-cut." They must enter an unpressurized, structurally unstable section of a derelict alone to retrieve a designated, high-value component. Upon their successful return, the Elders stamp their metal shoulder-plate with the ship's mark and bestow their *Deed-Name* in a ceremony accompanied by rhythmic hull-beating.
* **Temporary Unions:** Pairings among the Solder-Kin are contractual rather than permanent. Known as "welds," these unions are agreed upon for a set duration—typically three or five long-haul salvage runs. When the contract expires, the partners may choose to renew the weld or part ways amicably, dividing their shared salvage-shares according to the ship's ledger.
* **Dissolution (Death):** When a Solder-Kin dies, they are not cast into the cold void. Their tools and suit are stripped to be passed down to the next generation. Their physical body is placed within the ship’s reclamation tanks, where it is chemically dissolved to replenish the vessel's hydroponic systems and water reserves. To the Solder-Kin, this is the ultimate peace: physically sustaining the ship and the crew they loved.

### **Beliefs**
The Solder-Kin have completely forgotten the Golden Age. They live among the gargantuan ruins of ancient space-faring civilizations without any myths of who built them. To them, the universe has always been a graveyard of steel, and their holy purpose is to tidy it, recycle its bones, and keep the cold at bay.

They view the rising magic of the world—specifically the Gilded Shiver and the Shiver-Veneer—as a literal, infectious physical sickness. They believe it is a "gilded rot" that ruins good steel, corrupts navigation computers, and decays the minds of those who touch it. They use specialized chemical washes and salt-scrubs to keep their hulls pristine.

Their core creed is that **outsiders carry the rot**. They believe that those who dwell on planets, breathing "un-scrubbed air" and living under open skies, are spiritually contaminated and physically decaying.

### **Relations with Outsiders**
The Solder-Kin trade selectively, exchanging high-grade salvaged tech and refined metals for fuel, fresh soil-crates, and medicine. They do business with the Sovereign Reclamation Trust and make brief berths at Tether-End, but they rarely leave their ships. When they must interact with outsiders, they speak in a fast, clicking dialect known as **Borrowed-Speech**—a complex argot constructed from technical jargon, old navigation codes, and fragments of the Resonant Utterance.

Traditional friction exists with the independent salvage crews and corporate agents who do not respect the Solder-Kin's holy claim-marks on dead hulls along the Iron Ribbon.

### **Naming Conventions**
Solder-Kin do not use family names. They use earned *Deed-Names* that describe their role, a notable achievement, or a physical trait observed during their coming-of-age trial.

* **Grip-the-Valve**
* **Solder-True**
* **Rust-Sifter**
* **Bolt-Catcher**

### **Internal Tensions**
The Solder-Kin are currently facing a quiet, generational fault line known as **the Resource-Split**. The elder traditionalists believe that all salvaged materials must be hoarded, recycled, and kept within the fleet to maintain absolute self-sufficiency and protect the people from the "rot" of the outside world. 

The younger generation, however, is growing tired of recycled nutrient paste, stale air, and the constant threat of radiation sickness. They want to sell their high-grade salvage directly to the Ribbon-Way Consortium in exchange for fresh food, real spirits from the Butter-Burner, and modern engine parts, even if it means letting outsiders closer to their sacred hearths.
