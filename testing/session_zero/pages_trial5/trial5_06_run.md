## The Iron Ribbon

## Travel Overview
This transit carries the salvage tender away from the flickering safety of Tether-End and deep into the silent, debris-choked lanes of the Dead Corridor. The journey is defined by the constant hum of the ship’s hull and the anxiety of the fuel gauge. While the lane is relatively quiet, free from the aggressive raider patrols of the deeper sectors, the sheer density of drifting junk keeps the pilot's hands firmly on the thruster controls. The surrounding space does not feel empty; it feels like a graveyard waiting for new company. Yet, for a crew with a keen eye and a functioning crane, the drift is a treasure trove of frozen fuel-slurry, copper wiring, and abandoned emergency rations.

## Route Options

### The Low-Draw Lane
* **Time**: 6 Days
* **Vectoring DC**: 12, **Scavenging DC**: 10
* **Terrain Features**: Stable gravity eddies, ancient navigational buoys blinking weak amber warnings, and wide lanes of clear space.
* **Pros/Cons**: Highly predictable and easy on the thrusters, but the long transit time means a steady, unavoidable drain on the ship's life support and food rations. Salvage here is picked clean of high-value military grade scrap, leaving only basic raw materials.

### The Siphon Slew
* **Time**: 4 Days
* **Vectoring DC**: 17, **Scavenging DC**: 8
* **Terrain Features**: Dense kinetic debris fields, magnetic dust pockets that scramble short-range sensors, and tumbling hull-sections from old freighter collisions.
* **Pros/Cons**: A swift run that cuts days off the journey, packed with rich opportunities to siphon fuel and harvest pristine components. However, the dense debris makes hull scrapes highly likely, and a single navigation failure can send the ship spinning into a tumble.

## Encounters

### The Drift-Hedge
A massive, floating barrier of frozen polymer foam—the result of a long-ago freighter cargo rupture—blocks the lane. It is too wide to easily burn fuel going around, and the foam is dense enough to clog the tender's intake thrusters if they try to plow through. 
* *The Challenge*: The crew must find a way to clear a path. They can use the ship's mining lasers (risking overheating), attempt a delicate manual demolition during an EVA, or search for a natural gap in the foam using deep sensors.

### The Castaway's Transponder (Journey-Changer)
The ship’s receivers pick up a faint, automated distress beacon repeating on an old emergency frequency. The source is an intact escape pod from a long-dead passenger liner, wedged inside a hollowed-out asteroid. 
* *The Dilemma*: There are no living signatures inside, but the pod’s computer contains an intact, high-grade automated repair drone and an unencrypted log detailing a corporate betrayal that still affects the politics of Tether-End. Bringing the drone aboard offers massive maintenance benefits, but downloading the log could make the crew targets for powerful syndicates back at the station.

### The Scrapper's Claim
The tender stumbles upon a small, heavily patched-up salvage tug named the *Rust-Kicker*, currently tethered to a pristine military supply crate. The tug's crew is jumpy; their weapons systems are powered up but not locked on. 
* *The Encounter*: The captain of the *Rust-Kicker* warns the party to back off, claiming the salvage by right of discovery. The party can attempt to negotiate a trade, intimidate them into sharing, or take the cargo by force. A peaceful resolution might earn the crew a reliable contact in the deep drift, while violence will guarantee a hostile reception next time they meet.

## Discoveries

### The Frozen Tanker
A split-open chemical transport drifts in a slow, hypnotic spiral. Trapped within its ruptured cargo bay is a massive sphere of frozen deuterium ice—pure thruster fuel.
* *Temptation vs. Risk*: The fuel is incredibly easy to mine and would completely top off the ship's tanks. However, the tanker's structural frame is highly unstable. Any significant kinetic impact or careless thruster backwash could cause the wreckage to collapse, crushing the tender's salvage arms or trapping an EVA crew member.

### The Silent Corvette
An old military scout ship drifts dark and silent, its hull pristine save for a clean puncture wound through the engineering deck. 
* *Temptation vs. Risk*: The ship's forward armory is sealed and likely contains military-grade weapons and shield components. However, the corvette’s automated security network is still running on backup batteries. Bypassing the security door requires a difficult hacking attempt; failure triggers the ship's interior point-defense turrets, threatening to shred anyone inside the corridors.

## Journey Narrative Integration (Getting There is Half the Fun)
The journey is not merely a blank space between ports; it is the crucible in which the crew's trust is forged. 

During the third day of the transit, the ship’s primary air scrubber suffers a minor, non-critical failure. The air doesn't run out, but it turns thick, smelling of old copper, recycled tea, and damp wool. The crew is forced to work in uncomfortable close quarters while performing repairs. It is during this long, sweaty afternoon that the crew must decide how to handle their minor comforts. Do they crack open their one ration of real coffee to lift spirits, or do they hoard it for the hard work waiting at the salvage site? This quiet, domestic tension defines the trip far more than any asteroid field.

Later, while passing through a dust cloud, the crew witnesses the silent, breathtaking sight of a derelict passenger liner drifting parallel to them, its grand viewing gallery illuminated only by the cold light of distant stars. For a few hours, they run side-by-side with ghosts, a stark reminder of what happens to those who lose their footing in the corridor.

## Travel Moments: Camp, Night, Lost

### Setting Camp: Going Cold-Iron
To conserve precious fuel and reactor life during the long shifts between active navigation, the captain orders the ship to go "cold-iron" for eight hours. The main reactor is dialed down to a whisper, the gravity plates are lowered to a comfortable fraction of a G, and the heating systems are cut back. The crew huddles together in the galley, wrapped in heavy thermal blankets. The conversation is quiet, framed by the green glow of emergency consoles and the sound of their own breathing. It is a time for sharing old superstitions or quietly patching up gear by flashlight.

### The Night: The Tapping
During the quiet hours of the cold-iron drift, a rhythmic, metallic tapping echoes through the structural ribs of the living quarters. It sounds exactly like someone knocking on the outside of the hull with a wrench. Is it merely the thermal contraction of the metal plates cooling down in the shadow of a gas giant, or is something clinging to the sensor array? The tension on watch rises as the crew debates whether to ignore it or risk a freezing night-watch EVA to investigate.

### Getting Lost: Vector Drift
A sudden magnetic anomaly or a minor calculation error throws the ship off course, sending them drifting into an uncharted pocket of dead space where the navigation beacons are completely shielded. The sensor screens go flat gray. The realization that they are no longer on the mapped ribbon hits the crew with a cold weight. The pilot and navigator must work together to find their bearings using manual star-sights, all while the crew faces the mounting anxiety of burning extra fuel just to find the path they lost.

## Special Conditions
The Iron Ribbon is exceptionally rich in drifting detritus and abandoned cargo from historical shipping accidents. The drift is thick with usable scrap, frozen fuel pockets, and discarded supply crates. Finding resources is remarkably simple, allowing a clever crew to keep their tanks full and their hull patched using what they find along the way. The journey becomes less about surviving starvation and more about choosing which valuable finds to keep and which to leave behind.

## Mechanical Guidelines
* **Pace**: 
  * *Slow*: 2/3 travel speed, +4 to Vectoring checks.
  * *Medium*: Normal travel speed and checks.
  * *Fast*: 4/3 travel speed, -4 to Vectoring checks, no scavenging checks allowed.
* **Encounter Check**: Roll 6d6 once per day. Each die that rolls a 2 or lower triggers an encounter from the route list.
* **Discovery Check**: Roll 1d6 once per day. A roll of 3 or lower triggers a discovery.
* **Vectoring (Navigation)**: The pilot must make a Wisdom (Survival) or Intelligence (Vehicles) check against the route's Vectoring DC.
* **Scavenging (Foraging)**: The crew can make a Wisdom (Survival) or Intelligence (Technology) check against the Scavenging DC to find fuel, air refills, or spare parts.
* **Lost**: A failed Vectoring check means no progress is made that day. The crew must spend the day recalibrating sensors and burning 1 unit of fuel without moving closer to their destination.

## Story Hooks
* **The Ghost Signal**: A distress beacon that uses the voice of a crew member’s long-lost relative begins broadcasting from a nearby debris field.
* **The Toll Ghost**: A derelict automated defense platform blocks the lane, demanding a specific corporate clearance code or a payment of raw metal before it will deactivate its targeting locks.
* **The Stowaway's Cache**: A hidden compartment in the ship's own galley is discovered, containing a encrypted datapad that points to a massive, untouched military supply depot hidden in the Slew.
