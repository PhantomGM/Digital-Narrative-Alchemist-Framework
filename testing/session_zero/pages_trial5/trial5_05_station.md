### **Tether-End**

### 1. Overview
Tether-End is a cramped, metallic spark in the dark—a retrofitted modular waypoint floating at the edge of the void. Officially classified as a temporary deep-space staging platform, the station has outlived its original purpose by decades. Despite its modest physical footprint, it has become the undeniable heart of the sector's salvage operations. It is a place of stark contradictions: a structural skeleton that is slowly shaking itself apart, yet it remains the single most vital hub for anyone seeking to survive the surrounding graveyard of ships.

### 2. Physical Description
The station’s design is intensely utilitarian, laid out in a rigid, modular grid of interconnected cargo pods and decommissioned transport hulls welded directly to a central spine. There are no grand plazas or scenic viewing domes; every cubic meter of pressurized space is fiercely optimized. The exterior hull is a patchwork of mismatched armor plates and hasty weld-jobs, offering minimal defense against the drifting debris of the corridor. Inside, the public thoroughfares are narrow, pipe-lined conduits where travelers must walk single-file. The air is warm, smelling perpetually of ozone, scorched solder, and yeast from the hydroponic vats.

### 3. Population
The sheer density of bodies packed into these narrow corridors breeds a prickly, defensive attitude among the residents. Privacy is a myth, and the locals have developed a sharp, dark humor to cope with the lack of personal space. This constant physical proximity also means that respiratory ailments spread like wildfire through the vents, leaving much of the populace dealing with a damp, rattling cough known locally as "the Spigot wheeze." Despite the physical toll and the constant gray hum of exhaustion, a stubborn, resilient morale binds the community together. They are survivors, and they look out for their own.

### 4. Economy
A constant stream of raw hull-metal, unrefined fuel, and ancient technology flows into the station's cargo bays from the surrounding wrecks. This wealth of raw resources has shaped a workforce specialized in delicate atmospheric cutting and hazardous salvage. Because these skills are so rare and dangerous, the local Salvagers' Guild has grown incredibly powerful, dictating the terms of trade and squeezing independent crews. Very little of the actual wealth stays on the station, however; corporate tax-siphons and distant supply syndicates ensure that the residents live on a tight margin, trading in favors and recycled parts rather than hard currency.

### 5. Politics & Law
The official station administration is largely toothless, representing a distant corporate entity that rarely bothers to send updates, let alone resources. This hands-off approach leaves the community to manage its own external relations and disputes. Paradoxically, this lack of formal law has fostered a strange, transactional stability. Because everyone relies on everyone else for air, water, and power, disputes are settled through swift, pragmatic arbitration. Corruption is remarkably low, not out of moral purity, but because a dishonest mechanic or a thief simply cannot survive in a community where everyone’s survival is mutually assured.

### 6. Notable Locations
* **The Rusty Gasket:** The station's central galley and only watering hole. It is a noisy, warm space built inside a hollowed-out fuel bladder, where crews swap rumors, negotiate salvage rights, and drink a surprisingly palatable synthetic chicory broth.
* **The Sieve:** A massive, unshielded hangar deck where raw salvage is cataloged, stripped, and sorted. It is a chaotic hazard zone of swinging cranes, magnetic tethers, and sparks.
* **The Blind Pocket:** A labyrinth of maintenance shafts behind the main reactor grid. It is a poorly lit, unmonitored sanctuary where independent techs perform off-the-books ship modifications away from corporate eyes.

### 7. Surroundings
Tether-End floats in the absolute quiet of deep space, bordered on all sides by the drifting wreckage of ancient battles and failed colonization efforts. The immediate vicinity is a treacherous obstacle course of micro-meteoroids, frozen fuel pockets, and dead hulls. Beyond this debris field lies the vast, uncharted wilderness of the dead corridor, where the only light comes from the distant, cold stars.

### 8. Trajectory
The station's physical structure is slowly wearing out, its bulkheads groaning under the stress of too many docked vessels and aging atmospheric scrubbers. Yet, the population remains stubbornly packed into its metal shell, and the station's strategic importance is rising rapidly. As more salvagers push deeper into the corridor, Tether-End has become the only reliable port for parsecs—a vital lifeline that the sector cannot afford to lose, even as its foundations slowly decay.

### 9. Hooks & Opportunities
* **The Sieve's Silence:** A massive piece of salvage recently brought into the sorting bay has started emitting a low, rhythmic vibration that is disrupting the station's life support systems. The Guild is looking for an independent crew to board the wreck and shut down the source before the air scrubbers fail entirely.
* **Hobb's Ledger:** The station's cynical logistics officer has quietly approached the crew with a proposition: a corporate transport is arriving to collect the monthly resource quota, but a discrepancy in the cargo manifest could allow a valuable crate of medical supplies to "fall off" the dock—if someone is willing to handle the transport.
