### **The Resonant Utterance**

**Phonetic Patterns:**
*   **Consonant Tone:** Consonants are clear and distinct, providing a crisp, almost percussive frame for words, without being harsh or aggressive. Sounds are often released cleanly, allowing the following open vowel to carry the weight.
*   **Vowel Dominance:** The language is rich with long, open vowel sounds. Each word often centers around a prominent, sustained vowel, giving speech a drawn-out, almost sung quality. Diphthongs are rare, favoring pure, clear vowel tones.
*   **Complexity:** Words are predominantly monosyllabic or disyllabic. Each short word is a distinct, resonant utterance, packing significant meaning into its brevity. There is little compounding or agglutination.

**Naming Conventions:**
*   **NPC Names:** Names are short, resonant, and often evoke a single, powerful concept or natural element. They tend to be one or two syllables, rich with open vowels and clear consonant sounds.
    *   Examples: **Rian**, **Kael**, **Sora**, **Loam**, **Vaer**
    *   Rules: Names are typically monosyllabic or disyllabic, featuring prominent, open vowels. They often hint at a core quality or connection to simple, profound elements of existence, reflecting the individual's essence.
*   **Location Names:** Locations are named with short, evocative terms that highlight a singular, significant characteristic or a deeper, almost spiritual, resonance. These are often single resonant words or simple, clear pairings.
    *   Examples: **Deep-Awe**, **Sun-Glow**, **Still-Reach**, **Stone-Root**, **Void-Pillar**
    *   Rules: Names are composed of one or two resonant words, often hyphenated for clarity, each carrying a strong visual or conceptual weight. They emphasize a place's intrinsic nature or perceived purpose, often with a sense of ancient significance.
*   **Faction Names:** Factions bear names that are concise, impactful, and often abstract or symbolic. They convey the group's core identity or purpose through a resonant, meaningful phrase, giving them an air of ancient or profound authority.
    *   Examples: **The Echo**, **The Keen**, **The Loom**, **The Hearth-Keep**, **The Star-Singers**
    *   Rules: Faction names are typically one or two resonant words, often invoking abstract concepts, natural phenomena, or core values with a sense of ancient purpose and clarity.

**Common Idioms & Taboos:**
*   **Sayings:**
    1.  "Light is truth." (A simple, profound statement on clarity and honesty, often used in moments of discovery or revelation.)
    2.  "Hear the echo." (Encourages listening deeply, discerning hidden meanings, or considering the lasting consequences of actions.)
    3.  "Feel the void, but do not dwell." (A practical warning to acknowledge danger and emptiness without succumbing to despair, particularly relevant for salvagers.)
*   **Linguistic Taboos:**
    1.  Casual use of "The Great Silence" or "End-Breath": These terms refer to ultimate cessation or the absolute void of space and death. They are reserved for solemn rituals or dire pronouncements, never spoken lightly.
    2.  The "Whisper-Words": Certain ancient names or phrases, believed to hold primal power, are never spoken aloud outside of specific, highly guarded rituals, lest they invoke unintended consequences or draw unwanted attention.

**Influence on Other DNA:**
This language, with its short, resonant words and ritualistic tone, imbues communication with a profound sense of meaning. Military orders are delivered with crisp, singular words, each a potent command, carrying the weight of a declaration. Religious texts are not verbose but are chanted, each vowel-rich word a resonant note in a rhythmic, almost hypnotic sequence that builds a deep, spiritual atmosphere. Even casual conversation among the crew of The Gilded Shiver retains a measured cadence; thoughts are expressed with deliberation, each word chosen for its precise, resonant impact, lending an air of thoughtfulness and respect to everyday interactions. It's a language that makes even mundane observations feel significant.
